In one line: We teach AI to spot and follow small objects in orbit — satellites and debris — from a camera in space.

Why it matters

Orbits are getting crowded. A single collision can create thousands of new pieces of debris. Seeing objects early, from space, gives satellite operators time to act.

From a camera in orbit, a satellite can look like one faint dot among hundreds of stars. Our AI learns to tell the two apart and to follow each object from frame to frame.

What we are building

The project runs in three stages: Build (2026–2028), Fly (2029), Adapt (2030).

Work packageWhat it does
Work-A: AI-ready dataA physics-based image simulator, image enhancement, sim-to-real adaptation, and a labelled benchmark dataset — so the AI can learn before launch.
Work-B: Detection and trackingNeural networks that find faint objects among stars and link them across frames, plus a vision model adapted for space imagery.
Work-C: Post-launch adaptationAfter launch, update the AI with a small amount of real flight data.
